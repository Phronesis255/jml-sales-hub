          01

          Start with the right inputs.

          One strategy input pack for a human or AI strategist. Business facts first; content ideas second.

          01Client intake documents

          Capture what the business sells, its priority offers, service area, goals, differentiators and target audiences. Include existing social account links, account ownership, past activity and available analytics.

          • Separate established audience facts from assumptions.
          • Name the client contact, approver and community-management owner.
          • Record known campaigns, sensitive topics and factual claims that need client verification.

          Output: A clear business and audience brief, plus the current account baseline.

          02JML's 90-day plan or substitute brief

          Extract the priority business outcomes, offers, campaigns, dates and intended customer actions. Explain how social media contributes to that plan.

          If the plan is missing, use the five questions alongside this checklist. Reuse answers already supplied; ask only for the gaps.

          Output: Agreed priorities, or a clearly labeled substitute brief.

          03Social competitor analysis

          Record competitor account links, observation dates, platforms, positioning, content topics, formats, publishing patterns and visible audience response. Use SEMrush for research and APIFY to collect public competitor content with suitable Actors; confirm each tool’s platform coverage and preserve source links and collection dates.

          • Separate direct competitors from useful category examples.
          • Look for gaps the client can credibly address.
          • Visible engagement is an observation, not proof of sales or a reason to copy a competitor.

          Output: Evidence-backed opportunities and questions to investigate.

          04Client brand book

          Provide approved logos, colors, fonts, visual references, tone of voice and existing messaging rules. Link the actual asset files and identify anything missing.

          Resolve missing creative guidance before producing assets that depend on it. Give Claude and the designer the approved brand book, rather than relying on remembered brand details.

          Output: A usable reference for the strategist and designer.

          05Client media limitations

          Record existing usable photos, existing usable video, the ability to supply new photos and the ability to supply new video. Include access links, subject matter, quality, availability dates and known use permissions.

          Output: A realistic media plan. Explore the scenarios below.

          06JML production capacity & frequency limits

          Record the client's agreed deliverables and JML's maximum production capacity by format. Include video editing or other complexity constraints, approval lead times and revision limits.

          Distinguish unique assets from platform adaptations and publishing placements. A maximum is a ceiling, not a quota to fill.

          Output: The boundaries the calendar must respect.

          No 90-day plan?

          Complete this five-question substitute. It supplies direction without pretending a full business plan exists.

          1. What does the business sell, and which offer matters most over the next 90 days?
          2. Who are we trying to reach, and what problem or need brings them to the business?
          3. What should social media help achieve, and what action should people take?
          4. Why should someone choose this business, and what evidence supports that difference?
          5. Which promotions, launches, seasonal priorities or dates should shape the next 90 days?

          Ready for strategy: the business, audience, priorities, accounts, media and capacity are understood. Mark unknowns explicitly. AI may organize and propose; JML checks the evidence and resolves material gaps.

          03

          Give every platform a job.

          Use the input pack to make a small set of clear decisions before filling a calendar.

          Business priorityAudience needPlatform roleContent approach

          Where to show up

          Select Instagram, Facebook and/or LinkedIn based on the client's audience, objectives and resources. All three are eligible; all three are not mandatory.

          What to communicate

          Define the main message, evidence, content themes, formats and intended customer actions. Include relevant competitor observations and a current trend scan.

          How to judge progress

          Choose goal-relevant measures, record the baseline and identify the available data. Set a feasible cadence within the media and capacity limits.

          Strategy output and review checklist
          • Priority audience, business objective and desired action are explicit.
          • Each selected platform has a stated role and rationale. Research uses SEMrush, APIFY and native platform observation where appropriate.
          • Content themes, messages and claims are grounded in the business.
          • Formats and cadence fit the media scenario and JML capacity.
          • Measurement connects to the goal; primary KPIs, definitions, sources and targets are agreed, not invented. Use the goal-based scorecard in the Performance section.
          • Claude can synthesize the input pack and draft the strategy, calendar and briefs. JML verifies the evidence, reviews the work and obtains client alignment.
          04

          Approve the plan before production.

          Turn the strategy into a 30-day or 90-day calendar. Leave one or two intentional openings for timely, relevant content.

          Gate 1 · Calendar approval

          The client confirms topics, messages, formats, timing and planned media requests. Changes return to the strategist, then back to the client.

          Gate 2 · Creative approval

          Later, the client approves the actual assets and copy. An approved calendar or blank trend slot does not approve unseen creative work.

          Calendar fields and approval handling

          Give each content item a stable reference. Record its planned date, platform, objective, theme, topic, format, intended action, source media, dependencies and status. Add the designer brief and finished asset links as work progresses.

          • Name the client approver and agree the review deadline.
          • Keep feedback consolidated and identify which version is approved.
          • Pending approval stays pending. Reschedule affected items if needed.
          • Material changes to approved content return for approval.
          How trend slots move into production
          ObserveCheck relevanceFill the slotBrief & createApprove & publish

          Observe trends throughout delivery, with a check during weekly planning. Choose an idea only when it fits the audience, business goals, media resources and production window. Update the calendar and relevant briefs.

          If the idea requires new client media, issue a focused media brief. If no suitable trend appears, use an agreed evergreen alternative. Time sensitivity does not skip creative approval.

          05

          One calendar. Two production briefs.

          After calendar approval, turn the plan into clear instructions for the designer and, when needed, the client.

          Designer brief pack

          For the human or AI designer · required

          • Calendar reference, goal, platform and format.
          • Concept, message, copy or script, and intended action.
          • Brand direction and approved reference materials.
          • Source assets and outstanding client dependencies.
          • Required adaptations, exports and batch delivery date.

          Result: The designer knows what to make, why it exists and what is needed to finish it.

          Client media brief

          For the client · only when new media is needed and feasible

          • What to photograph or film, and which post needs it.
          • What to show, say or demonstrate.
          • Simple capture guidance and reference examples.
          • Required quantity, orientation and usable quality.
          • Delivery date, upload location and contact for questions.

          Result: The client has a specific, achievable request instead of “send us some content.”

          Check each item's readiness: briefs can be prepared in parallel. The designer may start items with usable inputs while others wait for client media. If a dependency fails, agree a substitute or reschedule the affected item.

          Keep the handoffs connected

          Use the same content reference in the calendar, designer brief, media request, review comments, final files and publishing record. Track status at item level: planned, awaiting media, in design, in review, approved, scheduled and published.

          Calendar confirmed: approved calendar + designer brief pack + conditional client media brief.

          06

          Produce a complete weekly batch.

          The default rhythm is weekly. Use a larger batch when it suits the client's needs and JML's capacity.

          Inside the Design Phase

          From approved brief to finished assets, with quality review and revision loops.

          1 · PREPARERead briefs and check source media
          2 · CREATEProduce and adapt the assets
          3 · REVIEWJML checks; designer revises
          4 · RELEASEClient approves; package the batch

          Designer responsibilities

          • Confirm the batch, deliverables and source files before production.
          • Flag missing media or unclear instructions against the relevant item.
          • Create posts, carousels, reels, stories or other agreed formats.
          • Adapt layouts and copy presentation for the selected platforms.
          • Return reviewable drafts with the correct calendar references.
          • Apply consolidated revisions and export the final versions.

          JML quality review

          • Does the creative fulfill the approved message and objective?
          • Are facts, names, offers, dates and claims correct?
          • Does it follow the client's brand book?
          • Are text, audio, subtitles and visual details clear?
          • Are platform versions, covers, captions and links complete?
          • Is the material suitable for the intended use and ready for client review?

          Design tools: Claude with Remotion or HyperFrames skills for motion graphics; Adobe Premiere for video editing; Adobe After Effects for motion design and compositing. Select the route that fits the brief, media and JML capacity. JML reviews the result regardless of how it was produced.

          Revision loop: a failed JML check returns to the designer. After it passes, client-requested changes return to the designer and through JML review again. Only the approved version moves to publishing.

          The publishing package

          Final approved assets, captions, covers where needed, links, tags, planned dates, platform instructions and approval status. Include a clear indication of which files and copy belong together.

          The designer may be human or AI. Claude, Remotion, HyperFrames, Adobe Premiere and Adobe After Effects support production; a named JML reviewer remains accountable for the batch.

          Meta's scheduling walkthrough

          LinkedIn Page admin roles

          Choose the personal-profile route at intake.

          Either the client publishes the prepared content package, or the client authorizes a compatible publishing integration to post on their behalf. Confirm the available formats and analytics before relying on that route.

          For client publication, agree the handoff date, obtain confirmation and the live post link, and arrange access to the performance data needed for review.

          LinkedIn member publishing authorization

          Before scheduling

          Correct account, version, caption, links, tags, date and time zone. Confirm approval.

          After publishing

          Open the live post. Check the actual media, crop, copy, links and publication status.

          If something fails

          Resolve the cause, confirm a revised time if needed, and avoid duplicate publication.

          Published means verified: record the live link and actual date. Scheduling success alone does not confirm publication. Comments, DMs and audience interactions remain with the client.

          08

          Measure the goal. Diagnose the gap.

          Choose the business outcome first. Then use supporting signals to understand why the content is, or is not, moving toward it.

          A different goal changes the scorecard.

          Agree one or two primary KPIs during strategy. Add a small set of diagnostic metrics, a baseline, a review window and a named data owner.

          Explore the review flowchart

          This selector is a reading aid. It does not set or save a client's strategy.

          Awareness: are more relevant people seeing us?

          Primary outcome

          Unique reach, using accounts reached or LinkedIn members reached where available. Check audience relevance using available demographics or audience breakdowns.

          Supporting signals

          Impressions or views, non-follower exposure where available, shares and video viewing behavior. These help explain distribution; views are not the same as unique people.

          Decision to make

          If relevant reach grows, continue the effective topic or distribution approach. If reach grows outside the intended audience, refine the topic, positioning or platform role.

          Read in: Meta Business Suite / Instagram Insights and LinkedIn's native analytics. Use JML's social media intelligence platform for consolidation once it is operational and validated.

          Consideration: is the content useful enough to act on?

          Primary indicators

          Choose saves, shares or substantive questions relevant to the strategy, with an agreed rate where a reliable denominator exists. These are indicators of consideration, not direct proof of trust.

          Supporting signals

          Relevant comments and replies, profile visits, repeat interest and video retention where available. Examine what people say, not only how many interactions appear.

          Decision to make

          Repeat useful formats and topics. If people see the content but do not save, share or ask relevant questions, improve its usefulness, evidence or clarity.

          Read in: native post analytics and available qualitative feedback. The client can share comment or DM themes; JML evaluates the signal without taking over replies.

          Website traffic: did attention become a useful visit?

          Primary outcome

          Social-attributed website sessions or engaged visits when website measurement is available. Otherwise report outbound link clicks as the available leading indicator.

          Supporting signals

          Outbound click-through rate, landing-page engagement and the intended next action. Keep outbound link clicks separate from clicks that only expand a post or open a profile.

          Decision to make

          Low clicks suggest a message or call-to-action issue. Strong clicks with weak website engagement suggest a mismatch between the post and the destination, or a tracking problem.

          Read in: native link analytics plus the client's website analytics, if supplied. Use consistent tagged links and verify the destination. Sessions and platform clicks will not necessarily match.

          Lead generation: did the content create qualified interest?

          Primary outcomes

          Qualified inquiries, completed lead submissions or booked discovery calls, according to the agreed lead definition. Deduplicate people and distinguish a new inquiry from a follow-up message.

          Supporting signals

          Relevant replies, DMs showing purchase intent, link clicks, landing-page conversions and qualification rate. Generic reactions, comments and total replies are not qualified leads.

          Decision to make

          Engagement without inquiries calls for a clearer offer or next step. Inquiries without fit suggest an audience or qualification issue. Qualified leads without bookings need a review of the client's follow-up and offer.

          Read in: native analytics plus the client's inquiry log, forms, booking system or CRM where available. The client supplies inquiry status and outcomes; community management and responses stay with the client.

          Sales or bookings: did interest become business?

          Primary outcomes

          Confirmed purchases, won deals or completed bookings attributable under the agreed method. Include attributed revenue only when reliable transaction data is available.

          Supporting signals

          Qualified opportunities, booking or checkout progression, conversion rate and lead-to-sale movement. Keep assisted influence separate from directly attributed results.

          Decision to make

          Trace the first weak step: attention, intent, inquiry, follow-up or purchase. Content changes address content problems; the client owns sales handling and customer follow-up.

          Read in: the client's sales, booking or CRM records alongside native post analytics. State the attribution window and method. If outcomes are unavailable, report that gap rather than infer sales from engagement.

          Lead-generation rule: prioritize qualified interest and downstream outcomes. Replies and engagement help explain the path to a lead; they become lead evidence only when they meet the agreed qualification criteria.

          Inside the Performance Review

          A repeatable weekly review, with enough detail to make the next content decision.

          1 · ALIGNConfirm the goal and primary KPI
          2 · VALIDATECollect and check the evidence
          3 · DIAGNOSECompare like-for-like content
          4 · ACTUpdate briefs and record a decision

          1. Confirm what success means this week.

          Read the approved strategy and tag each content item with its goal, theme, format, platform and intended action. Confirm the primary KPI, definition, data source and comparison period before judging results.

          Use client-specific targets or a baseline when agreed. Do not introduce universal engagement benchmarks or change the success measure after seeing the results.

          2. Collect delivery, platform and business evidence.

          Start with the publishing log: planned items, published items, live links, actual dates and delays. Export the available post and account analytics from Meta Business Suite, Instagram Insights and LinkedIn.

          For lead or sales goals, obtain the client's inquiry and outcome records. Match them to content or campaigns only where the evidence supports the link. Preserve collection dates and the original metric labels.

          Tools now: native analytics and source exports, with Claude assisting synthesis after JML checks the inputs. Planned: JML's proprietary social media intelligence platform, once operational and checked against native totals. It is not operational yet.

          3. Decide whether the evidence is ready.

          • Confirm account, date range, time zone, publication status and extraction date.
          • Compare posts at a similar age. Do not compare a one-day-old post with a mature month's total as if exposure were equal.
          • Separate organic from paid or boosted results where the breakdown is available; identify mixed totals when it is not.
          • Check for missing data, duplicate content records, changed metric definitions and unusually small samples.
          • If evidence is insufficient, label the conclusion provisional and set the next check. Missing data is not zero performance.

          4. Compare within the right context.

          Compare the same platform, objective and format where possible. Review topic, opening hook, creative treatment, length, call to action and media source. Use both post-level results and the appropriate account-level reporting period.

          Use medians or distributions alongside exceptional winners when the sample allows. A single viral post or weak post should not determine the whole strategy. Public competitor engagement can suggest hypotheses, but it does not reveal competitors' private reach, lead quality or sales.

          Research tools: SEMrush for competitor and topic research, and APIFY for collecting public competitor content and visible activity with suitable Actors. Keep source links, dates and coverage limits. Use JML's intelligence platform once operational; native client analytics remain the reference for owned-account results.

          5. Find the first weak step.

          Observed patternInvestigateNext content decision
          Low relevant reachAudience fit, topic, hook, format and distribution.Test one meaningful change to how the content earns attention.
          Reach, but little useful engagementValue, specificity, proof and creative clarity.Improve the useful takeaway or the way it is presented.
          Engagement, but few clicks or inquiriesOffer relevance, next-step clarity and destination.Strengthen the connection between the content and the desired action.
          Inquiries, but low qualificationAudience, offer framing and lead criteria.Make fit, context and expectations clearer.
          Qualified inquiries, but few salesClient follow-up, booking process, offer and sales handling.Separate content changes from actions the client needs to own.

          6. Record one usable decision per finding.

          Continue

          Repeat a topic or format when comparable evidence supports it.

          Improve

          Change the hook, edit, message, proof or call to action.

          Test

          State the hypothesis, change, success measure and review window.

          Retire

          Reduce an approach when enough evidence supports stopping it.

          Log the observation, evidence link, interpretation, confidence, action, owner and next review date. Update the designer brief or calendar item. Material changes to already approved content return to the client.

          Close the loop: weekly decisions improve the next batch. At month-end, the same verified evidence feeds an internal report and a client-facing report. JML reviews Claude's summaries and calculations before either report is used.
          KPI definitions, formulas and counting rules
          MeasureDefinition or calculationKeep consistent
          ReachDistinct accounts or members reached, as defined by the platform.Do not sum post reach or platform reach and label it a deduplicated audience total.
          Impressions / viewsThe platform's reported exposure or viewing count.Keep its original label and definition. Repeat exposures or plays may be included.
          Engagement rateAgreed interactions ÷ stated denominator × 100.List included actions and whether the denominator is reach, impressions or followers. Do not compare unlike definitions.
          Outbound CTROutbound link clicks ÷ impressions × 100, when both are available for the same scope.Do not substitute all-clicks or profile-clicks counts. Label a reach-based click rate separately.
          Lead qualification rateQualified unique inquiries ÷ all eligible unique inquiries × 100.Use the same inquiry cohort, qualification criteria and follow-up window.
          Landing-page lead conversionAttributed lead submissions ÷ eligible landing-page sessions × 100.Only calculate when website measurement and the attribution scope are available and consistent.
          Booking or sales conversionConfirmed outcomes ÷ the agreed eligible lead or session cohort × 100.Name the denominator, conversion window and attribution method. Deduplicate and allow time for the outcome.

          Report the numerator and denominator with each rate. Use N/A when a required denominator is zero or unavailable. A valid count of zero is different from a missing measurement. Keep directly attributed and assisted outcomes separate.

          Set up the measurement agreement at intake
          • Business goal, intended action and one or two primary KPIs.
          • Metric definitions, lead qualification rules and counting exclusions.
          • Baseline, client-specific target if agreed, and comparison period.
          • Source access, client data owner and collection cadence.
          • Link-tagging approach and attribution window where relevant.
          • How missing data, small samples and client follow-up delays will be reported.

          For inquiries handled in comments or DMs, the client supplies a concise outcome log or agreed export. JML measures the results; the client continues to handle the conversation.

          Weekly output: a verified KPI snapshot and an actionable decision log. Use these to generate the two monthly reports.

          Internal performance report

          For the JML team · operational detail

          • Planned versus delivered work, delays, capacity and publishing issues.
          • KPI definitions, source exports, date ranges and data-quality limitations.
          • Per-post and theme/format analysis, with relevant comparisons.
          • Experiments, hypotheses, confidence and what to continue or retire.
          • Production bottlenecks, media dependencies and client follow-up gaps.
          • Next actions with owners, deadlines and changes to briefs or the calendar.

          Purpose: improve how the team produces content and what it chooses to produce next.

          Client-facing results report

          For the client · outcomes and next decisions

          • A plain-English summary tied to the agreed business goal.
          • Primary KPI results against the baseline, prior period or agreed target.
          • Selected examples explaining the main wins and shortfalls.
          • Lead or sales outcomes where verified, with attribution limits stated.
          • What JML will continue, change or test next.
          • Decisions, approvals or new media needed from the client.

          Purpose: explain the value, the evidence and the next plan without overwhelming the client with production detail.

          Monthly reporting procedure and tools
          1. Close the reporting period. Confirm the account, time zone, comparison dates and extraction date. Reconcile the publishing log with native analytics and client outcome records.
          2. Generate both drafts. Use Claude to help synthesize the verified evidence. Link each material claim or calculation to its source. The internal report keeps the full analysis; the client version selects the relevant conclusions.
          3. Review inside JML. Check totals, definitions, rates, comparisons and attribution. Separate observations from hypotheses and list material gaps.
          4. Deliver the client report. The JML lead sends the reviewed version and requests the specific decisions needed for the next period.
          5. Update the working plan. Assign internal actions, revise upcoming briefs and obtain approval for material calendar changes.

          Current reporting inputs: Meta Business Suite, Instagram / Facebook and LinkedIn native analytics, publishing records and the client's available lead or sales records. Planned consolidation: JML's proprietary social media intelligence platform once operational and validated. Reporting continues through the current sources until then.

          Same evidence, different detail: both reports must reconcile to the same KPI definitions and reporting period. Client-friendly presentation must not hide a material shortfall, missing data or attribution limitation.
